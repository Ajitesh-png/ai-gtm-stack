# Case study: what 11 posts taught us about X

*A full-population teardown of a founder's X account, July–August 2026, that
became the `x-content-playbook` skill. Numbers are the measured public metrics
of those posts. The account is anonymised.*

## The question

The account had posted 11 originals in two months. Some reached over a million
views. Some barely cleared a thousand. The team's intuition about why was
strong, confident, and, as it turned out, mostly about the wrong variable. We
pulled every original with views, replies and bookmarks and ranked them three
ways.

## Finding 1: the three metrics have three different levers, and they are nearly independent

| Metric | What moved it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Views | a news peg or a category-killer first line | the top three posts (1.88M, 1.35M, 1.32M) all rode a platform launch or a labour reframe |
| Replies | a "Comment [WORD]" gate | remove the gate and replies collapse to single or low double digits, **even at 1M+ views** |
| Bookmarks | a named, portable move | saves clustered on posts that taught something reusable (147, 93, 1,581) |

The implication reorganised how posts get written: **pick the target metric
before the first line.** A post that does not know which of the three it is
chasing gets none of them.

## Finding 2: replies are engineered, not earned

Three teardown posts pulled 200K to 1M views each and single-digit replies. The
content was strong; the posts simply never asked. Adding "Comment TEARDOWN and
I'll send the full breakdown" costs the hook nothing and converts lurkers into
attributable leads. Every gate word now gets a payload built before the post
runs, and a distinct word per post so replies can be traced.

## Finding 3: the hook does about 90% of the work

Every winner opened with one of four families: a bold reframe, a metaphor with
a curiosity gap, an in-scene story, or a contrarian reversal. The body rarely
changed the outcome. The contrarian reversal was the cheapest reach in the set.

## Finding 4: craft does not buy reach

One founder-philosophy essay, well written and sincere, did 914 views against a
median around 465K. No customer outcome, no visual, no persona, no "you"
benefit up top, no CTA. On this account, essays run only behind an outcome hook
and a picture, or as a reply under a post that is already performing.

## Finding 5: the product is a character

The best posts referred to the product by name and pronoun, as a hire with a
day in its life, not as a feature set. Personified software is a story;
features are a list. Signature refrains that repeated verbatim across posts
earned reposts on their own.

## What changed

- Every post is tagged with a target metric before drafting.
- Teardowns carry a gate; launches carry a gate and an offer.
- Angle families rotate; no two of a kind in a row.
- A news-peg post ships within 24 hours of every major platform moment.
- A gap over six days in the calendar is treated as a defect.
- The rules are dated and expire: the teardown re-runs each quarter.

The full rule set, with the pre-publish checklist and calendar rules, is the
skill at `skills/x-content-playbook/SKILL.md`.
